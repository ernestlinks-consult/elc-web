How to plan your study abroad journey, month by month

A realistic timeline from your first shortlist to your flight, and what to gather at each stage.

Most students who miss an intake don't fail on grades. They run out of time: a transcript takes weeks, a reference never arrives, or a visa appointment is booked too late. Planning backwards from your start date fixes that.

12 months before: decide where and what

Pick your level, field and two or three countries. Be honest about budget: tuition, living costs and the proof of funds a visa will ask for. Check if your passport is valid; if not, start it now.

9–10 months before: build your documents kit

  • Official transcripts and certificates
  • Two references, from lecturers or employers
  • A CV and a first draft of your statement of purpose
  • An English test date, if your schools need one
Not sure where you stand? Start online in under a minute and Peace will message you with a plan, free. Start online

7–8 months before: apply

Apply to a realistic mix of programmes. Many funded places and scholarships close earlier than ordinary admissions, so check Scholarship Hunt for deadlines.

4–6 months before: offers, deposit and funding

Compare offers on total cost, not just tuition. Pay the deposit only once you're sure, and keep every receipt: you'll need them for the visa.

2–3 months before: visa

Once the school sends your confirmation document, prepare the visa: forms, financial documents, biometrics and, for some countries, an interview. See how we help with visas.

The last month: travel

Book flights after the visa is granted, arrange your first weeks of accommodation and keep copies of every document in your hand luggage.

Rules and deadlines change. We confirm the current requirements for your country and programme before you apply.
